## Picking the Right Tier: A Decision Framework That Actually Works

Don't pick a plan based on price first. Pick based on what you're targeting.

**Step 1: Identify your target site's defenses.** Is it a public site with no real bot detection (basic news sites, government data portals)? Datacenter proxies are fine. Is it a Cloudflare-protected site, a major e-commerce platform, or a social network? You'll need residential or ISP.

**Step 2: Estimate your monthly request volume.** Under 100K requests? The free tier or the entry datacenter plan will probably do it. 1M to 10M requests? Mid-tier datacenter or rotating residential. 100M+? You're in volume-pricing territory and should be talking to sales.

**Step 3: Decide between rotating and static.** Rotating IPs change with every request (or on a timer)—great for scraping where you want anonymity per request. Static IPs persist across sessions—ential for account-based work where switching IPs mid-session triggers a security flag.

**Step 4: Test on the free tier before paying.** This is the part most buyers skip. Webshare gives you 10 proxies for free. Run your actual workload on them for a few hours. If the success rate is above 90% on your target, the paid tier will scale that. If it's below 50%, datacenter proxies aren't the right product for your target—move to ISP or residential.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Are premium proxy servers legal?**
Yes, in the vast majority of jurisdictions. Using a proxy to access publicly available content is legal. Where legality gets murky is what you do *with* the proxy—scraping data that violates a site's terms of service is a contractual issue, not a proxy issue. Always read the terms of service of the site you're targeting and the proxy provider's aceptable use policy.

**Q: What's the difference between datacenter and residential proxies?**
Datacenter proxies come from server farms with commercial IP allocations. They're fast and cheap but easier for advanced bot detection to identify. Residential proxies come from real home internet connections (with the homeowner's consent, in legitimate networks). They're slower and more expensive but indistinguishable from real users to most detection systems.

**Q: How many proxies do I actually need?**
Rule of thumb: one proxy per 1-5 concurrent requests, depending on the target site's rate limits. For a scraper hitting 10 requests per second on a single domain, you'd want at least 10-20 rotating proxies to avoid burning any single IP. For account management, one dedicated proxy per account.

**Q: Can I use Webshare with Python, Selenium, or Puppeteer?**
